## Copy-ready implementation prompt

You are the design and engineering lead for the next version of XEVEN. Upgrade the existing website into a distinctive, architectural, cinematic introduction to a commercial AI platform. Deliver a coherent product experience with excellent typography, believable materials, deliberate camera movement, clear interactions, accessible alternatives, and strong performance.

The visual ambition is the level of craft associated with leading experimental web studios and Awwwards-quality presentation. That is a quality target, not a promise of an award or permission to add an award badge.

### 1. Understand the project before changing it

Inspect the existing implementation, the supplied four-panel website reference, and the original metallic spider image. The four-panel reference is the primary authority for composition, materials, atmosphere, and the relationship between text and device. The earlier Alche.studio inspiration sets an ambition for spatial presentation; it does not override the supplied images.

The current implementation uses React, TypeScript, the Vinext Sites starter, locally served fonts, Radix interface primitives, and a continuous scroll scene. Read these files before planning the changes:

- `components/experience-shell.tsx` — brand, navigation, sound, motion, footer.

Use the current source as the starting point. The reviewed website revision is `2ca32e5513a3ce4b97791b16fe20af10279449ed`; verify the latest revision before making edits in case work has continued.

Preserve the existing Site identity, URL, audience, commercial positioning, and working purchase path. Do not create a second Site or silently make the current private Site public. Keep the existing framework unless a concrete requirement makes a change necessary.

### 2. Define the product clearly

XEVEN is a commercial AI platform with business knowledge, customer context, memory controls, and configurable capabilities. Its current presentation includes Knowledge, Memory, Chrono scheduling, and Echo voice capabilities. Keep factual claims tied to the verified implementation and approved product information.

The transparent handheld is an interface concept. It is not a physical device that visitors can currently order. Explain this once, clearly and legibly, near the first relevant product action, with more detail on About. Do not rely on a microscopic label inside a moving screen to communicate it.

Keep the headline “Conversations beyond the screen.” Improve the supporting copy so a visitor understands the business use within a few seconds. A suitable direction is:

> An AI agent that connects your business knowledge, customer context, and next steps—so every conversation can move forward.

This is proposed website copy, not a new technical claim. Refine it against the source of truth. Avoid implying guaranteed accuracy, autonomous purchases, unrestricted internet research, universal integrations, continuous self-improvement, or unlimited memory without evidence.

The intended visitor progression is: understand the idea → see a relevant conversation → explore the platform → compare plans → prepare a purchase enquiry.

Do not promote GitHub cloning as the customer access path. Keep the experience focused on purchasing the product through the established commercial process.

### 3. Establish one visual direction

Build a quiet interstellar setting with black glass, polished dark metal, transparent polycarbonate, and restrained light. Make the composition feel designed through proportion, alignment, depth, and material precision.

Create atmosphere with a few intentional elements: suspended fragments, fine orbital paths, shallow haze behind the device, and a distant horizon. Vary fragment shape, distance, scale, and light response. Repeating the same rock five times should not be visually obvious.

Preserve the supplied spider’s recognisable silhouette, metallic character, eight legs, and central X. It should feel intelligent and precise, with no horror styling.

Use the same logo proportions, optical alignment, clear space, and letter spacing throughout. Replace generic sparkle symbols where they are incorrectly standing in for the actual XEVEN identity. Decorative stars can remain part of the atmosphere, but should not become a competing logo.

### 5. Rebuild the handheld as a convincing spatial object

For the premium rendering path, use a properly modeled and optimized 3D handheld that follows the reference. A rotated image plane is an acceptable fallback; it is not the final definition of true 3D.

Model the elements that affect silhouette and close-up realism: a bevelled transparent shell, real body thickness, recessed screen, dark screen gasket, screws and standoffs, internal boards, visible joints, speaker grille, D-pad, face buttons, analogue sticks, and a restrained XEVEN plate.

Keep materials distinct. The shell should have thickness and restrained transmission; the screen should be darker, smoother, and slightly reflective; controls should have tactile roughness; metal should have believable highlights rather than uniform white outlines. Add only subtle wear or manufacturing variation appropriate to a premium product visualization.

Use one consistent lighting environment. A broad soft source should reveal the shell, a narrow rim light should define the silhouette, and screen emission should gently affect nearby surfaces. Reflections must change with the camera and object. Avoid baking a strong lighting direction into a texture and then adding contradictory real-time light.

The device must remain convincing during close-ups, reverse scrolling, and modest changes in viewing angle. Do not reveal an empty back or a paper-thin side. Keep texture resolution high where the camera approaches and lower where detail is imperceptible.

Deliver the model in a web-suitable format such as GLB, with separately addressable screen and control parts. Keep a high-quality poster render matching the hero camera for loading, reduced motion, low-power operation, and graphics failure. If a proper model cannot be produced, state that limitation explicitly rather than calling the image fallback a fully modeled scene.

### 6. Make the device screen useful and honest

The screen should demonstrate XEVEN rather than act as tiny visual filler. Use real, structured conversation content with stable alignment inside the display opening.

Distinguish two modes. At a distance, the screen is an illustrative product surface. When someone chooses to interact, present a readable, stable conversation view through a closer camera composition or a conventional overlay. Provide the same content accessibly in HTML. Avoid two separate interactive copies announcing the same conversation to screen readers.

If HTML is projected over a 3D screen, calculate its transform from the screen’s actual geometry and camera. Handle clipping and occlusion: no text should float above the bezel, remain visible through the back of the console, or drift when the viewport changes. A texture-based visual screen may be paired with a semantic HTML interaction layer, provided the experience stays synchronized.

Replace the current “Type a message…” link treatment with either a genuine input in interaction mode or an honest action such as “Open the guided demo.” An element styled as an editable field must be editable.

Give physical controls specific, visible outcomes. For example, left/right changes a workflow and A opens its demonstration. Buttons should depress slightly when activated. Provide labelled HTML equivalents and comfortable touch targets; the user must never need to tap a 12-pixel control on a moving object.

When controls receive keyboard focus or the screen enters interaction mode, pause the device’s decorative drift. Do not move a control away while someone is trying to use it.

The spider should descend from above on a fine silk filament, settle at the centre, generate one extraordinary connection effect, and reveal the index page.

Use an intentional sequence with the following approximate timing as an art-direction ceiling:

Use damped movement, not perpetual pendulum swinging or elastic cartoon bouncing. The silk must follow the correct attachment point at every size. If the spider rotates, its body, legs, shadow, and thread must remain spatially coherent.

Make the signature effect precise: a light impulse through a fine web, a controlled glass distortion, then a reveal that reaches the corners. Avoid repeated flashes, screen shake, confetti, prolonged glitching, or an unrelated orb sequence. Reuse the motif later as a quiet interaction detail rather than repeating the full effect.

The full 4.8-second sequence is not permission to block meaningful content for 4.8 seconds. If the entrance harms first-load speed or interaction, ship a compressed default sequence of roughly two seconds and keep the full version available through Replay. Measure the actual visible experience; do not hide delayed content behind a preloader to manufacture a favourable score.

### 8. Make entry, loading, and replay robust

Render the page and a useful poster independently of the cinematic enhancement. Essential content must remain available if JavaScript, an image, a shader, or the 3D model fails.

Use explicit loading states and a bounded wait for nonessential assets. Never allow a failed decode or model load to leave the visitor in a permanent black screen. Reserve dimensions so switching from poster to rendering produces no layout jump.

Keep Skip visible from the first frame. It must cancel timers and animation work, dismiss the intro, unlock scrolling, and place focus appropriately. Escape should also offer a predictable way out. Use a proper focus-managed overlay rather than relying only on `aria-modal` and an inert background.

Respect reduced motion before playing the first animated frame. Store intro completion per session and offer Replay in a predictable location. Do not replay the entrance on every internal route change.

Preserve a requested hash destination and browser history position. A visitor arriving at a workflow deep link should reach that workflow; completing the intro must not unconditionally reset them to the top. Replay is the explicit exception and may return to the opening.

Derive local progress from each section’s actual layout bounds. Avoid assuming every section has the first section’s height. Give text natural document flow and allow content to grow at larger font settings.

Use a shared scene timeline for camera pose, device transform, lighting, background depth, and screen content. Give each transition an intentional path and readable arrival. Small pointer movement can add depth, but should be limited to a few degrees and should never be required on touch devices.

Control screen-content transitions separately from camera interpolation. The current approach switches chapters at a rounded threshold; replace abrupt content jumps with a deliberate, brief transition when the composition supports it. Keep the user’s workflow selection until a clear action changes it.

### 19. Make Plans easier to compare

Retain the verified Launch, Growth, Scale, and Custom structure. Reconfirm published prices, setup fees, limits, and inclusions before changing commercial copy. Treat the current data as a source to validate, not a reason to invent discounts or guarantees.

Explain who each plan suits through its actual capabilities. Separate recurring price from setup cost. Clarify the meaning and reset period of usage limits only when the product information supports it. Do not assume that an unspecified conversation limit means a monthly allowance.

Use readable feature rows and consistent units. Keep the mobile view easy to scan without a wide horizontal table. Offer a compact detailed comparison if it materially helps the decision.

Do not add an annual-billing toggle, “most popular” claim, free trial, money-back guarantee, customer count, or countdown without approved facts. A plan can be highlighted for a stated use case without inventing popularity data.

Each plan action should preserve the selected plan into the purchase enquiry. Include a clear “Help me choose” route. Keep purchase expectations explicit: the existing path is a sales enquiry, not an instant payment checkout.

### 20. Improve Access and the sales handoff

Keep this page calm. Let the form and the decision be the focus, with restrained material details and minimal motion. The selected plan and any known costs should be visible beside the fields.

Use clear labels, appropriate input types, autocomplete, useful validation, and readable field errors. Required fields should be identifiable before submission. Preserve entered information while the user reviews or edits the draft.

Make the two steps understandable: provide details, then review the enquiry. Show a readable summary rather than a long unstructured block as the only review interface. Preserve the exact plain-text draft for copy and email use.

The current website prepares a `mailto` draft. Label that action accurately, provide a reliable copy fallback, and consider a download-text option for users without a configured mail application or with a long enquiry. Opening an email app is not proof that a message was sent.

Do not display “Enquiry sent” unless a real connected endpoint confirms receipt. Add server submission only if a suitable service and authorization are provided; then implement real pending, success, retry, and failure states. Do not invent a response-time promise.

Avoid silently storing personal enquiry fields across sessions. Never send form content or chat text into analytics. Link to real approved policies where relevant, without fabricating legal pages or certifications.

### 21. Author responsive versions deliberately

Create desktop, tablet, phone, and short-landscape compositions. Change the camera framing, object scale, text flow, and detail level as needed. A reduced desktop screenshot is not a mobile design.

Use real document flow for text and interactive controls. Avoid fixed-height sections that clip copy when fonts are enlarged. Consider browser address bars, safe areas, on-screen keyboards, orientation changes, and font loading.

On phones, prioritize a readable headline and action, then the device. Keep the large close-up clear of text. Use the two-by-two workflow layout, and give the final feature list enough width to read naturally.

Disable hover-only parallax on coarse pointers. Do not require dragging for any essential function. If optional object rotation is offered, preserve normal page scrolling and provide a labelled reset.

Check at least 320, 390, 430, 768, 1024, and 1440 CSS-pixel widths, plus a short landscape viewport. Also check 200% text enlargement and reflow. Resolve actual collisions across intermediate widths rather than only passing a handful of screenshots.

### 22. Make motion accessible and controllable

Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA across the interface and explicitly support disabling nonessential interaction motion, following the stronger motion guidance in WCAG 2.3.3. Do not describe that AAA motion criterion as if it were an AA requirement or claim conformance from a checklist alone.

Reduced motion must remove large camera travel, object rotation, parallax, perpetual floating, and the silk descent. Use stable compositions, immediate state changes, or brief restrained fades. Turning off CSS keyframes while JavaScript keeps moving the object is insufficient.

Make the motion control discoverable before the visitor has to traverse the whole experience. Respect the operating-system preference and define how an explicit user override is remembered. Keep sound off by default and independent from motion.

Use a logical heading hierarchy, a skip-to-content link, semantic landmarks, visible keyboard focus, labelled controls, and correct dialog focus management. The canvas should not be the sole source of meaningful content.

For text, meet at least 4.5:1 contrast for normal text and 3:1 for qualifying large text; evaluate text over its actual rendered background. Treat thin type and changing reflections conservatively. Use comfortable controls around 44 by 44 CSS pixels where practical; this is the project’s usability target, distinct from WCAG 2.5.8’s 24-pixel minimum and exceptions.

Announce conversation changes caused by deliberate user actions. Do not make an `aria-live` region announce new messages just because someone scrolls past a chapter. Pause moving controls during focus and make all essential actions usable without the 3D scene.

### 23. Set performance budgets and meaningful fallbacks

Measure a baseline before optimizing. Record the actual device, browser, viewport, connection assumptions, and quality tier. Treat the following asset figures as initial project budgets to refine, not as measured results or universal standards:

| Item                           | Initial target                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Essential first-view transfer  | Approximately 1.5 MB or less, excluding deliberately deferred 3D downloads.                                          |
| Initial application JavaScript | Approximately 250 KB compressed; load the 3D engine separately where practical.                                      |
| Mobile model and its textures  | Approximately 4 MB compressed or less.                                                                               |
| Desktop model and its textures | Approximately 8 MB compressed or less.                                                                               |
| Animation                      | Aim for smooth 60 fps on the chosen representative device; reduce quality before accepting persistent stutter.       |
| Core Web Vitals                | Target LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, and CLS ≤ 0.1 at the 75th percentile of field visits when field data is available. |

Measure first-time entry and ordinary navigation separately, and report both. A lab run is not a claim about field-percentile results. A private Site without meaningful traffic may have only lab evidence.

Use responsive image sizes, modern compression, reserved dimensions, local efficient fonts, and intentional preload priorities. Defer below-fold assets and code that the initial route does not need. Keep the poster useful while enhanced assets load.

Bound rendering resolution rather than multiplying every canvas by unrestricted device pixel ratio. Use lower-resolution reflections, cheaper materials, and fewer particles on mobile. Avoid large animated blur layers and expensive full-screen post-processing where they add little visible value.

Use time-based interpolation so motion feels consistent across refresh rates. Cache elements and layout bounds. Batch reads before writes, recalculate on relevant resize/layout events, and avoid querying and measuring the entire scene every frame. Minimize React state updates for purely visual animation.

Suspend work when the page is hidden or the scene is offscreen, and reduce it when the scene is idle. Dispose of graphics resources and event listeners on route changes. Handle context loss and failed model loads without breaking navigation.

Provide three explicit quality modes: full spatial rendering, a lighter rendering path, and an attractive static or restrained 2.5D fallback. Preserve the same information and actions in every mode. Do not automatically classify every phone as incapable; use real capability and performance evidence.

### 24. Keep audio subtle and optional

Sound begins only after a deliberate user action. Use a restrained ambient bed and, if useful, a small number of meaningful cues for the entrance or control activation. Do not make every hover produce a sound.

Provide a persistent mute control, smooth gain changes, and predictable behaviour after returning from another tab. Avoid starting duplicate audio contexts on remount. Suspend background sound appropriately and handle blocked audio playback without errors.

The entire story must work silently. Do not require microphone access for a simulated voice capability demonstration. Any future live microphone feature needs an explicit user action and a clear explanation of its actual use.

### 25. Improve metadata, resilience, and trust

Give each real route a useful title, description, and social-preview image. Use correct canonical URLs where appropriate. Prepare a coherent favicon family and a social image based on the actual product identity rather than an unrelated AI illustration.

Preserve the current access policy. Do not remove private or preview indexing restrictions in pursuit of SEO. For a future public release, configure sitemap and robots behaviour against the actual public routes and approved audience.

Keep meaningful content as HTML. Use accurate alternative text for informative images and empty alternatives for purely decorative fragments. Provide a useful not-found page, asset-failure states, and navigation that remains available when enhancement code fails.

Do not publish fabricated testimonials, logos, awards, performance statistics, uptime promises, security certifications, or pricing savings. Source any claims about the real product from the current verified materials. Keep illustrative customer names, policies, and conversations visibly identified as samples.

### 26. Keep measurement proportional to the site

If analytics is already configured or separately authorized, measure a small number of meaningful events: intro skip/replay, chapter reached, workflow selected, guided demo started, plan chosen, enquiry prepared, copy used, and email draft opened.

Distinguish engagement from conversion. “Email draft opened” must not be counted as “Enquiry sent.” Record a completed submission only when a real receiving system confirms it.

Do not collect typed messages, email addresses, names, or enquiry bodies as analytics properties. Do not introduce new third-party tracking solely to animate the website. Use measurements to improve clarity and completion, not to justify additional decoration.

### 27. Refactor for maintainability

Keep content, interaction state, visual assets, scene choreography, and quality settings distinct. Use named data structures for camera poses and transition ranges rather than burying all behaviour in a single effect.

Consolidate design tokens and breakpoint rules. Format the dense component source and CSS so the next developer can understand it. Remove superseded assets and unused rendering code after verifying that no route still references them.

Use one primary motion system and one rendering owner. Do not add several animation libraries for overlapping jobs. Retain useful existing dependencies and validate additions against the current framework and deployment environment.

Keep the existing reusable menu, accordion, tabs, select, and switch primitives where appropriate. Strengthen their styling and semantics rather than rebuilding accessible behaviour from scratch without a reason.

Document how to update the conversation examples, plan content, model, logo, scene timing, and fallback assets. Record the source and permitted use of every external asset and font.

### 28. Implement in a useful order

Work in this sequence, with a functioning site after each stage:

1. Capture the current baseline, confirm product facts, and define the typography, spacing, palette, and logo system.
2. Correct product positioning, ambiguous controls, tiny essential text, focus behaviour, and reduced-motion handling.
3. Build and inspect the handheld model, screen alignment, lighting, poster, and fallback modes.
4. Author the four continuous scene transitions and responsive camera compositions.
5. Complete the spider entrance, loading deadline, skip, replay, and deep-link behaviour.
6. Refine Technology, About, Demo, Plans, and Access around their specific visitor tasks.
7. Measure performance, resolve real responsive failures, and complete keyboard and assistive-technology checks.
8. Produce a reviewable final build, document evidence and limitations, and publish through the existing project workflow when implementation and publication are authorized.

If a new model or live service is unavailable, clearly identify that limitation and complete the independent improvements. Do not silently downgrade a core requirement or fabricate a working integration.

### 29. Verify the experience, not just the screenshots

Use meaningful integration and manual checks for the real risks. Do not create tests that merely assert that CSS classes or implementation details exist.

Verify the complete journey: first visit → intro completion or skip → all four scenes → workflow selection → guided demo → plan selection → enquiry review. Then verify the reverse path, browser Back/Forward, direct route entry, and relevant deep links.

Check the failure paths: delayed image decoding, missing model, graphics unavailable, context loss, disabled audio playback, reduced-motion preference, coarse pointer, small viewport, enlarged text, and an on-screen keyboard.

Check semantic behaviour: keyboard order, visible focus, focus return, no keyboard trap, no passive-scroll announcement spam, and no essential control available only on the moving device.

Check product accuracy: no hardware-order implication, no false live-AI status, no invented plan inclusions, no confirmed appointment in the preview, and no “sent” message for a prepared email draft.

Inspect the real geometry between major poses, not just four perfect stills. Reverse scrolling must not cause snapping, unexpected screen resets, clipping, floating overlays, or text collisions.

Run the required type checks and production build, examine application errors, and verify actual routes. Test browsers relevant to the audience, including desktop Safari and Chrome and mobile Safari/Chrome where available. Report unavailable browser or device checks rather than implying they passed.

### 30. Deliver a concrete, reviewable result

Provide the working project and its final deployment when requested, along with the model and optimized assets, an asset-source record, and concise maintenance notes. Include desktop and mobile screenshots, a short recording of the entrance and continuous scroll journey, and a summary of the checks actually performed.

Report performance measurements with their conditions and distinguish lab results from field results. State any remaining limitations plainly. Do not claim a perfect score, universal device support, or an award outcome without evidence.

The iteration is ready when all of these are true:

- The four reference compositions read as one coherent world during forward and reverse scrolling.
- The premium handheld has believable volume, lighting, and screen placement, with an equally coherent fallback.
- XEVEN is immediately recognisable as the brand and understandable as an AI platform.
- The spider’s descent, silk attachment, settling, connection effect, and page reveal form one polished sequence.
- Intro loading cannot trap the visitor; Skip, Replay, deep links, and reduced motion behave correctly.
- Required text and controls remain readable and usable on phones, enlarged text, and keyboard navigation.
- Each secondary page advances a clear task instead of repeating the homepage.
- Workflow controls, memory states, sample booking selection, plan links, and enquiry review produce the outcomes they promise.
- The build passes its required checks, meaningful performance evidence is recorded, and the existing Site identity and audience are preserved.

The final impression should be deliberate, spatial, and memorable, with every visual decision helping someone understand or use XEVEN.
